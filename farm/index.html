Operations

Weeder goose programme, suspended

Notice 2025-11 · posted 2 June 2025

Geese have been put to work weeding row crops for well over a century. They prefer grass to broadleaf and will walk a field all day for nothing but the grass. Ours took to it immediately.

They cleared the strawberry bed in a single morning, including the strawberries. The programme is suspended pending a definition of the word "weed" on which both parties can agree.

Guard rota

Notice 2024-07 · posted 30 August 2024

Geese have guarded distilleries and bonded warehouses for decades and are, by most measures, better at it than an alarm system. They do not sleep through anything and they cannot be bribed.

Ours were assigned the west gate in 2024. They have since extended the post to cover the lane, the car park and the office door. No unauthorised person has entered this property since. On eleven occasions, neither has an authorised one.

Egg yield, projected against actual

Notice 2026-05 · posted 11 May 2026

The prospectus assumed three hundred eggs per bird per year. That figure is correct, and it is correct for hens.

A goose lays between twenty and forty, in spring, and only if she judges the year to have gone well. The prospectus has been amended. Investors were notified. Two replied.

Safety Notices

Feeding policy

Notice 2025-03 · posted 8 February 2025

Bread offers a goose no nutrition of any kind and can deform the wing of a growing bird permanently. Feeding bread on this property is therefore prohibited.

One exemption is on file. It is held by the liaison post, where the terms describe it as a stipend. The exemption predates the policy and cannot be withdrawn without reopening the matter for discussion.

Bereavement

Notice 2025-12 · posted 21 October 2025

Geese pair for life and will mourn a lost mate, in some cases for a full season. Where this occurs the bird is excused from the guard rota and from weeding for as long as it takes.

No form is required. We are told when it has happened.

Imprinting, 2024 intake

Notice 2026-03 · posted 2 April 2026

A gosling imprints on the first thing it sees move. In the spring of 2024 that was the previous liaison.

The consequences were not anticipated by the policy and are not covered by it. The birds concerned are two years old now. They still attend.
